1. The Pulse — Kick on the Downbeats

In 4/4 time the strongest beats are 1 and 3. Placing the kick there creates a solid foundation. Feel how the low thump anchors everything.

2. Classic Rock Beat

Kick on 1 & 3, snare on 2 & 4 (the “backbeat”), and steady 8th-note hi-hats. This is the backbone of countless rock and pop songs.

3. Four-on-the-Floor

Kick on every quarter note (1, 2, 3, 4). Common in disco, house, and EDM. The constant pulse makes people want to dance.

4. Hip-Hop / Boom-Bap

Kick and snare with a slightly swung feel. Hi-hats often play 16th notes. The space between hits is as important as the hits themselves.

5. Funky 16ths

More syncopation — kicks and snares off the main beats, open hats for color. Great for practicing independence and groove.

Practice tip: Load a pattern → Play it a few times → Clear the sequencer → Try to rebuild it from memory. Then change the tempo and notice how the feel changes.

What is a Beat?

  • 4/4 time — four quarter-note beats per bar (the most common).
  • Beat 1 is the strongest (downbeat). Beat 3 is next. Beats 2 & 4 are the backbeats.
  • The sequencer shows 16 steps = one bar of 16th notes.
  • Steps 1, 5, 9, 13 = the four quarter notes.

Drum Roles

  • Kick — low end, pulse, “heart” of the groove.
  • Snare — sharp crack, usually on the backbeat (2 & 4).
  • Hi-hat — timekeeper, adds motion and texture.
  • Open hat — airy accent, often on the “and” of 4 or off-beats.
  • Tom / Clap — color, fills, and emphasis.

Note Values (in 4/4)

  • Quarter note — one beat (4 per bar).
  • 8th note — half a beat (8 per bar).
  • 16th note — quarter of a beat (16 per bar) — what each sequencer step is.
